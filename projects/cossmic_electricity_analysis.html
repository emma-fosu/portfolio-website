Project Overview

Project Overview

Household electricity consumption has a direct impact on both cost savings and environmental sustainability. To reduce costs and support Germany's sustainability policies, household electricity consumption needs to be studied to identify ways to improve efficiency.

This project explores the consumption patterns of 11 households in Southern Germany across different times of day and seasons to better understand their usage behavior. The goal is to identify opportunities for reducing wastage and optimizing energy efficiency.

Key Questions Addressed

  1. What are the daily, weekly, and seasonal electricity consumption patterns of households?
  2. At what times of day do households experience peak electricity usage?
  3. How does appliance-level usage contribute to overall household consumption, and which appliances consume the most energy?
  4. What proportion of household electricity demand is met by on-site solar generation, and when does shortfall occur?
  5. Which days or periods show the highest potential for energy savings?
  6. How does household electricity usage compare to solar generation patterns across different times and seasons?
  7. Which strategies (behavioral changes, appliance upgrades, or solar usage) could most effectively reduce energy consumption and reliance on the grid?

Key Findings

NOTE: These findings are based mostly on 2016 electricity consumption analysis.
  1. Electricity Consumption Peaks and Valleys throughout Seasons.

    Electricity consumption varies dramatically across the year. It peaks in winter (Nov-Jan), then gradually decreases through late winter to early spring.

    In April, it drops sharply by 40% compared to March, reaching its lowest point in summer (78% below the beginning of the year). From September onwards, consumption slowly increases, with a spike from autumn to early winter, reaching 3x what was consumed in August.

  2. Daily Electricity Patterns Remain Stable but Peak in Winter.

    Electricity use throughout the day is generally consistent. Occasional peaks occur likely when appliances like the dish washer or heat pump are in use. Winter consumption is clearly higher than in summer and autumn, with noticeable peaks around 4-5 AM, 10 AM, and after sunset.

  3. Heat Pumps Dominate Winter Energy Use.

    The heat pump is the primary driver of winter electricity consumption, accounting for 72%, with the circulation pump next in line.

    During spring and autumn, the heat pump competes with other appliances, contributing around 24%, alongside the dishwasher and washing machine. This indicates that winter spikes are largely due to heating needs.

  4. Solar PV Generation Exceeds Demand in Summer and Autumn.

    Solar electricity generation surpasses household demand and sometimes grid import during summer and autumn. Starting in March, PV generation exceeded demand by 82%, averaging 370% above demand over the next four months, with a peak of 910% in July.

    Much of this surplus is wasted, highlighting the untapped potential of solar energy. Effective storage or flexible usage strategies could maximize this energy during peak generation periods.

  5. Solar Electricity is Highly Predictable Throughout the Day.

    Solar PV generation follows a consistent daily pattern: it rises from 8 AM to noon and falls after 3 PM until sunset. While the timing is predictable, the magnitude varies seasonally, with the highest output in July.

  6. Electricity Demand and Solar Generation Follow Opposite Trends.

    Electricity consumption and solar generation behave like two opposing parabolic curves. In winter, solar generation is far below demand, while in July it surpasses demand by 910%. Demand increases toward the end of the year, but solar generation cannot meet it, emphasizing the need for alternative energy sources or storage solutions to bridge the gap.

Recommendations

  1. Implement Hybrid Electricity Management with Smart Switching:

    Introduce a hybrid electricity system with automated switching: the system prioritizes PV solar electricity when generation is high and seamlessly draws from the grid only when solar is insufficient.

  2. Connect PV Solar Electricity to Low-Power Appliances:

    Directly connect PV-generated electricity to Low-power appliances (LED lights, fans, chargers, small pumps) which are ideal for consuming this surplus energy without affecting household operations.

  3. Implement Energy Storage or Demand-Shifting Strategies:

    Invest in battery storage systems to store excess solar energy during high-generation months for use in winter or during peak demand periods.

    Alternatively, implement demand-shifting strategies, such as scheduling flexible appliances (washing machines, dishwashers, chargers) to run during peak solar generation hours (8 AM-3 PM), maximizing self-consumption and reducing grid dependency.

Dataset

A time series collected by CoSSMic (Collaborating Smart Solar-powered Microgrids). More infomation about the dataset can be found here.

Methodology

Data Cleaning & Preparation (with Power Query)

  1. Columns relating to industrial areas and public infrastructure were removed.
  2. The columns were indexed with Power Query index creator.
  3. In order to calculate the differential electricity consumption of each appliance, the table was duplicated with an offset index of 1.
  4. These tables were merged by the index and each record was substracted from their respective cumulative appliance consumption.
  5. Differential electricity consumption was computed by indexing
  6. Total appliance electricity consumption from the household was calculated to create five new columns (refrigerator, dishwasher, freezer, heat pump, cooling pump).

Exploratory Data Analysis (with Pivot Tables and Charts)

  1. Created Pivot tables to show the total electricity against time.
  2. Created a Pivot table to show electricity usage throughout the day.
  3. Computed the percentage usage of each appliance electricity consumption.
  4. Calculated the total solar (PV) electricity generated, grid electricity imports and exports.
  5. Calculated the portion of grid import consumed at a specific time.

Visualization & Dashboards (with Pivot Charts and Sliders)

  1. Created a time-series line plot of total electricity consumption against time (years, months, quarters).
  2. Created a treemap to show the portion of electricity consumed by each appliance.
  3. A timestamp slider to filter out all the charts and tables.
  4. Designed a new card to show total electricity consumption, total grid import and export and pv import.

Skills Showcased

  • Using Power Query with the M language to load the dataset, compute new fields and remove columns.
  • Pivot tables with computed measures and formatting to analyse electricity consumption.
  • Pivot charts to visualize time series electricity consumption and appliance usage.
